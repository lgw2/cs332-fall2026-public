\subsection*{Problem from Prairie Learn}
Momo is planning a road trip along a highway with $n$ mile markers, numbered
from $1$ through $n$. Momo is currently at mile marker $1$, and she would like
to travel to mile marker $n$. However, Momo has a very low attention span and
gets exhausted easily, so she can only drive 1 or 2 miles forward on a single
day.

Luckily, there is a hotel right next to each mile marker; the costs of these
hotels are provided in the array $HotelCosts[1..n]$. If Momo stops at mile $i$
at the end of the day, she will stay overnight in a hotel that costs
$HotelCosts[i]$. Determine the minimum possible total cost of all the hotels
that Momo stays in during her road trip.

Momo does not have to stay in a hotel at mile $1$ or mile $n$, so
$HotelCosts[1]=HotelCosts[n]=0$. She cannot stay in the same hotel two nights
in a row. For example, if $HotelCosts=[0,1,374,3,0]$, an optimal trip stops at
miles 2 and 4, for a total cost of $1+3=4$.
\begin{itemize}
    \item 
\textbf{English recursive subproblem definition.} Let
$MinCost(i)$ denote the minimum possible cost of traveling from mile
to mile 1 to mile $i$ , including the cost of the hotel at mile $i$.
\item
\textbf{Recurrence.} The trip to mile $i$ must come from mile $i-1$ or
 mile $i-2$. Thus,
\[
MinCost(i)=
\begin{cases}
0 & \text{if } i=1,\\
HotelCosts[2] & \text{if } i=2,\\
HotelCosts[i]+\min\{MinCost(i-1),MinCost(i-2)\} & \text{if } 3\leq i\leq n.
\end{cases}
\]
\item 
\textbf{Memoized algorithm.} 
\begin{algo}
ComputeMinTotalCost(array $HotelCosts[1..n]$):\+\\
	let array $MinCost[1..n]$\\
	$MinCost[1] = 0$\\
	$MinCost[2] = HotelCosts[2]$\\
	for $i = 3$ to $n$:\+\\
		$MinCost[i] = HotelCosts[i] + \min\{MinCost[i-1], MinCost[i-2]\}$\\
	\-\\
	return $MinCost[n]$\-\\
\end{algo}
\end{itemize}
